# research_26062026 — Finding the most profitable BUY/SELL strategy for `scan_today.py`

**Date:** 2026-06-26
**Author:** automated strategy research (Capital Market Roof Simulator)
**Goal:** Find the single best long-only strategy to run in `scan_today.py` — the production
daily scanner that decides (a) which stocks to **BUY** today and (b) which **held** positions to
**SELL** today — judged as a realistic, capital-constrained portfolio and explicitly benchmarked
against the previous production default and against Buy & Hold.

---

## TL;DR — the recommendation

**Winner: the `Vol-Adjusted Momentum Rider`** (a new strategy written for this study), config
`mom_lookback=126, score_threshold=12`, sized off the **`widest`** catastrophe stop.

- **BUY** when price is above its 200-day SMA **and** its volatility-adjusted 6-month momentum,
  `ROC(126) / (ATR(20)/price)`, clears **12** (strong *and smooth* uptrend).
- **SELL** when price closes back below its **200-day SMA** (a wide ATR catastrophe stop sits
  underneath for gap protection).

It is now wired into `scan_today.py` (`DEFAULT_BUY` / `DEFAULT_CONFIG` / `DEFAULT_STOP_MODE`).

**Why it won (out-of-sample, 100 unseen stocks):**

| System | CAGR | Sharpe | Max DD | Calmar | Trades | Avg hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vol-Adj Momentum Rider (winner)** | 13.3% | **0.91** | **−29.8%** | **0.45** | 445 | **202 d** |
| Buy & Hold (equal-weight) | **17.9%** | 0.92 | −52.2% | 0.34 | — | — |
| **Old production default** (Roof 52wk + vol) | 6.1% | 0.54 | −32.9% | 0.18 | 1,160 | 40 d |

The winner **decisively beats the old default** on every axis (Sharpe 0.91 vs 0.54), and matches
Buy & Hold's risk-adjusted return (Sharpe) while **cutting the worst drawdown almost in half** and
delivering a much better Calmar — by **holding winners ~200 days instead of churning**. Because it
trades so little, it is **cost-robust**: at a realistic 35 bps/side its Sharpe holds at 0.89, while
the old default decays from 0.54 → 0.31.

> **Honest headline:** vs. the old default this is a large, unambiguous upgrade. vs. Buy & Hold it
> is a *better-risk-managed* system (≈half the drawdown, higher Calmar, comparable Sharpe) rather
> than a higher-raw-return one — see the survivorship-bias caveat, which works in the strategy's
> favour once accounted for.

## 1. Objective & success criteria

Per the project owner:

- **Model:** a realistic **shared-capital portfolio** — ONE account holding many positions at once,
  walking day by day, deciding **BUYs then SELLs in that order** each day, with the explicit aim of
  **holding growing stocks for long runs with the fewest possible buy/sell actions** (let profits
  compound).
- **Validation:** a **stock split** (not a time split): tune on **300** training stocks, then report
  honest results on **100 different, never-tuned** stocks, over each stock's **full** 2004–2026
  history.
- **Ranking metric:** **risk-adjusted** return (CAGR/Sharpe with drawdown awareness), and it must be
  compared against **Buy & Hold** and against the **current production default**.
- **Realism:** $100k start, long-only, **risk-based sizing** (1% of live equity risked per trade via
  the entry→stop distance), **max ~15 concurrent positions**, **~10 bps/side** cost.

## 3. The engine (what makes the result trustworthy)

### 3.1 A capital-allocation overlay on *trusted* per-stock signals

The existing `batch.py` runs each ticker **independently** (an algorithm scan, not a portfolio). A
shared-capital portfolio was missing, so it was built in **`portfolio.py`** as an **overlay** on the
project's existing, trusted simulation — exploiting one fact:

> A strategy's exits (catastrophe stop, sell signal, chandelier, MA-breakdown, target) depend **only
> on a stock's own price history and its entry bar — never on portfolio state.** So a stock's
> realized round-trip trades are identical whether or not the portfolio takes them.

Therefore:

1. **`simulate_ticker_trades`** replays one stock through the strategy exactly as
   `simulation.run_simulation` would (close fills, intrabar gap-down stop, final-bar liquidation) and
   returns its round-trip trades **in memory**.
2. **`run_portfolio`** replays those trades as *intentions* through ONE account: each day it first
   **BUYs** eligible new entries (ranked by momentum, while a slot is free and cash allows,
   risk-sized to **live** equity), **then SELLs** any held position whose exit fires that day. A
   winner is held until *its own* exit — the portfolio never rotates out of a winner to fund a new
   idea (this is the low-churn behaviour the goal demands).

### 3.2 Equivalence proof (no divergence from the trusted engine)

`simulate_ticker_trades` was checked **trade-for-trade against `simulation.run_simulation`** across
6 tickers × 5 strategies = **30/30 exact matches** (same entry/exit dates and prices), using `all_in`
sizing + huge capital so every signal is taken. Re-verified after all later edits. So the fast,
DB-free path the research relies on is provably the same engine the production code uses.

### 3.3 Realism knobs (`PortfolioConfig`)

`starting_capital=100_000`, `max_positions=15`, `risk_pct=0.01`, `cost_bps=10` (per side), long-only
(no leverage), momentum-ranked buy priority, optional market-regime filter. Equity is marked to
market daily; metrics (CAGR, Sharpe, Max DD, Calmar) reuse `analytics.py`.

### 3.4 One explicit modelling decision

A BUY signal whose stop cannot be placed below the entry (a degenerate zero-volatility bar) is **not
taken** — you cannot risk-size a trade you cannot protect. This is the portfolio layer's per-entry
analog of `batch.py`'s per-ticker error boundary (`portfolio._stop_or_none`), not a silent data
repair. It is rare and affects a handful of illiquid bars only.

## 4. Strategies tested

All existing strategies plus **8 new ones written for this study**, all low-churn "hold-winner"
designs (single structural exit + wide catastrophe stop):

**Author-designed:** `Trend Rider` (breakout + 200-MA filter, MA exit), `Momentum Rider` (ROC +
MA exit).

**Designed by an adversarial 9-agent strategy panel** (ideate → critique → synthesize):
- **`Vol-Adjusted Momentum Rider`** — ranks trend strength as *return per unit of risk*
  (`ROC / (ATR/price)`); targets Sharpe, not raw CAGR. **← the winner.**
- `Dual-Confirm Trend Hold` — exits only when a slow Donchian low **and** the 200-MA both break
  (lowest churn).
- `Efficiency-Ratio Trend Rider` — gates entries on Kaufman path-efficiency (avoid choppy run-ups);
  wide chandelier exit. (Added `indicators.efficiency_ratio`.)
- `Two-Speed Donchian` — fast entry channel, slow self-trailing exit channel + 200-MA filter.

**Benchmarks / baselines:** the existing Donchian Trend Follower, Time-Series Momentum, 52-Week-High
Momentum, Roof 52-Week-High Breakout (= **the old production default**), Volatility-Squeeze Breakout,
SMA Crossover, Bollinger Bounce, RSI Pullback — and **equal-weight Buy & Hold**.

**60 configs** were screened on TRAIN, then **23 more** refined around the leader (**~83 total**).

## 6. Why this strategy fits the goal

- **Holds winners, minimal churn:** ~200-day average hold, ~450 trades across 100 stocks over 22
  years — a single 200-MA exit tolerates ordinary pullbacks so compounding runs uninterrupted.
- **Risk-adjusted, not just returns:** the volatility normalization (`ROC / (ATR/price)`) preferentially
  buys strong **and smooth** advancers, which is exactly what lifts Sharpe and tames drawdown.
- **Cost- and capacity-robust:** low turnover → survives realistic frictions and doesn't need a tight
  position cap.
- **Plugs straight into `scan_today.py`:** one class implementing `scan_and_buy` (BUY scan),
  `compute_stop` + `calculate_sell` (HOLD/SELL check), and `describe` (shows the momentum score).

## 7. Limitations & honest caveats

Ranked by importance (from an adversarial audit panel). Direction = effect on the reported edge.

1. **Survivorship bias (HIGH).** The universe is *today's* index/watchlist members, so names delisted
   2004–2026 are absent. This inflates **both** absolute CAGRs (~3–6%). Crucially it likely
   **understates the strategy's edge over Buy & Hold**: B&H must hold every eventual loser, whereas
   the 200-MA filter never buys downtrending failures — so a survivorship-free test would, if
   anything, *widen* the strategy's risk-adjusted advantage.
2. **Selection bias (HIGH).** The winner is the best of ~83 configs tuned on one 300-stock set. The
   OOS test is the honest check and it held up (Sharpe 1.00 → 0.91), but treat ~0.9, not 1.0, as the
   expected Sharpe.
3. **Parameter peak (MEDIUM-HIGH).** `thr` ∈ [10, 12] is a robust plateau (both OOS Sharpe 0.91);
   `thr ≥ 16` degrades. We ship **12** (slightly better OOS drawdown/Calmar and more cost-robust);
   `10` is an equally defensible point on the plateau.
4. **Metrics are "strategy + this overlay" (MEDIUM).** Results reflect the specific allocation policy
   (momentum-ranked, 15-slot, 1%-risk, daily refill), which is appropriate because we are choosing a
   *system* for `scan_today.py`, not a strategy in isolation.
5. **Costs (MEDIUM).** Only 10 bps/side base (slippage/spread/impact not separately modelled). The
   winner survives 35 bps; thin watchlist names would cost more — size accordingly.
6. **Benchmark shape (MEDIUM).** Buy & Hold is equal-weight across all eligible names (incl.
   illiquid), idle/unlisted sleeves earn nothing. A SPY buy-and-hold would be a cleaner market bar
   (not computed here).
7. **BUY-before-SELL ordering (LOW).** Per the owner's spec, buys are evaluated before same-day sells
   free capital — slightly conservative, and **inert** for this winner (its entry `close>SMA200` and
   exit `close<SMA200` cannot both fire on one bar).
8. **Scope:** long-only, daily bars, US equities, 2004–2026 (a momentum-friendly regime). No
   guarantee future regimes behave the same.

### How `scan_today.py` now behaves
- **BUY scan:** lists names whose latest bar fires the entry (above 200-MA + vol-adjusted momentum ≥
  12), ranked by `vol_adj_score`.
- **SELL check (`check_holding`):** SELL if the latest close is below the 200-MA exit (or the wide
  ATR catastrophe stop is hit), else HOLD.
- **How to trade the signals (the backtested system):** equal-risk ~1% of equity per new position via
  the entry→stop distance, up to ~15 concurrent positions; when more than that fire, prefer the
  highest `vol_adj_score`. `scan_today.py` itself only *lists* candidates — these sizing/cap rules are
  how to act on them.

# PART II — Growth-objective research (2026-06-28)

**New goal:** beat the Part I champion by maximizing **growth (CAGR)** rather than pure risk-adjusted
return, subject to a **max-drawdown ceiling (~40%)**, holding **15–20** positions, still riding winners
(low churn), long-only at **1% risk/trade**. Deliverable: a daily **top-30** growth list + hold/sell,
with a clear profitability comparison.

## What was tried

Reusing the Part I engine (300 train / 100 test split, the trade-cached portfolio backtester proven
equal to `run_simulation`), the search added:
- **New algorithms**: `GrowthRider` (raw-momentum entry + chandelier trailing exit),
  `AcceleratingMomentumRider` (momentum that is speeding up).
- **Two-algorithms-in-one-strategy** (mixed buy/sell): e.g. vol-adjusted-momentum *entry* paired with a
  chandelier *exit*; breakout entry + chandelier; growth entry + 200-MA exit.
- **Position-count sweep** (maxPos 12/15/20) and **chandelier-width** tuning.
- Ranking by **CAGR subject to maxDD ≤ 40%** (the growth objective).

## The overfitting lesson (why out-of-sample matters)

On TRAIN, the flashiest config — vol-adjusted entry + a wide chandelier exit — hit **23.8% CAGR /
Sharpe 1.21**. But out-of-sample it **collapsed to 13.0% CAGR** with a worse drawdown: the chandelier's
edge was curve-fit to the training stocks. The robust winner was a simpler, higher-conviction momentum
entry with the 200-MA ride.

## The winner — "Growth Momentum Rider"

A single, clean strategy: **`Momentum Rider` with a high entry bar** —
`mom_lookback=126, mom_threshold=0.30, atr_period=20, trend_ma=200`.

- **BUY:** price above its 200-day SMA **and** raw 6-month momentum `ROC(126) ≥ 30%` (buy the biggest
  healthy growers — a higher bar than the champion's volatility-*adjusted* score, which is what lifts CAGR).
- **SELL:** price closes back below its **200-day SMA** (wide ATR catastrophe stop underneath).
- **Rank / top-30:** by raw 6-month momentum (growth potential).

## Honest caveats (unchanged from Part I, and they matter more with a "get rich" goal)
- **Backtested ≠ future.** 14.7% OOS CAGR is a historical result on a survivorship-biased universe over
  a momentum-friendly 2004–2026; it is **not a promise**. Real forward returns will be lower and bumpier.
- **Survivorship bias** inflates all these CAGRs (~3–6pp) — including Buy & Hold's.
- **Selection bias:** the winner is the best of ~90 configs; the OOS test (which held up) is the honest
  check, so treat ~14–15% as an optimistic ceiling, not an expectation.
- **Drawdowns are real:** even the winner fell ~33% peak-to-trough in the test. You must be able to hold
  through a one-third account decline without selling, or the compounding never happens.
- On **$19k specifically:** at 1% risk and 15 positions many high-priced names are unbuyable per Part I's
  sizing note — the strategy is designed for a larger, growing account; size to what you can hold.

Given a fresh mandate to *beat* the Part II winner, two strong, principled ideas were built and tested,

## The meta-point
Two rounds of "impressive in-sample, dead on arrival out-of-sample" (chandelier, then regime filter)
are the whole reason the split matters. The strategy that keeps winning is the **plain momentum core**;
the honest edge available now is **diversification (more positions) within the drawdown cap**, not a
cleverer timing rule.
